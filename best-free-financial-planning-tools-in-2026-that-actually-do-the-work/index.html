1. Empower (formerly Personal Capital) — Best Overall Free Financial Dashboard

Empower is the most complete free financial dashboard on this list. Connect your accounts — checking, savings, investments, loans — and see your whole financial life in one place. Empower says its dashboard and financial tools are completely free.

What it does for free:

  • Net worth tracking across all linked accounts
  • Retirement Planner for test-driving retirement scenarios
  • Portfolio analysis
  • Budgeting and cash flow
  • Savings planner, debt paydown and emergency fund tools

The retirement planner is the standout. Enter your savings, expected contributions and retirement date, plan for big life events, and see a projection that compares a median outcome with a lower-bound one — a much more honest picture than a single number.

The catch: Empower also sells paid wealth management, and its Personal Strategy advisory service is pitched to people investing more than $250,000. You can use the free tools and decline the advisory offer.

Best for: Anyone who wants a complete financial picture in one place.

2. ProjectionLab — Best for Detailed Financial Modeling

ProjectionLab lets you build detailed projections of your financial future, from now through retirement, with more control over assumptions than most planning tools.

What the free Basic plan does:

  • Forecast your complete financial timeline from now to retirement
  • Monte Carlo simulations
  • Historical backtesting — stress test your plan against past markets
  • Flexible modeling of income, savings and spending

What you pay for: compare mode, what-if scenarios, multiple plans, Roth conversions, tax optimization, withdrawal strategies and cash-flow projections are part of Premium, at $129 a year. The Pro plan ($549 a year) is built for financial advisors managing clients, not for individuals.

Best for: Detail-oriented planners who want to model their long-term path and are willing to pay if they need side-by-side scenario comparison.

3. Boldin (formerly NewRetirement) — Best Retirement Planning Tool

Boldin is built specifically for retirement planning and models retirement in more depth than any other tool in this category — but most of that depth is in the paid plan.

What the paid PlannerPlus plan adds:

  • Social Security Explorer — compare claiming ages for your situation
  • Roth Conversion Explorer
  • Healthcare cost estimates in retirement
  • Lifetime tax planning with federal and state tax projections
  • Stress testing and Monte Carlo analysis

Why it stands out: most tools give you a single retirement number. Boldin models the moving parts of an actual retirement — Social Security timing, healthcare costs, taxes and conversions.

Price: the Basic plan is free and lets you start a plan. PlannerPlus costs $168 a year, billed annually after a 14-day free trial.

Best for: Anyone within 10–20 years of retirement who wants serious planning and is willing to pay for it.

4. Monarch Money — Best for Couples and Ongoing Planning

Monarch Money is technically a budgeting app, but its planning features are strong enough to include here. Net worth tracking, goal planning and cash flow work well together.

What it does:

  • Net worth tracking
  • Goal-based savings planning with progress tracking
  • Cash flow forecasting
  • Collaborative features for couples
  • Investment account tracking

Price: there is no free plan. The Core plan costs $14.99 a month or $99.99 a year, and the first week is free. It is the only tool here with no free version, included for its planning depth.

Best for: Couples or individuals who want budgeting and planning in one app.

5. Vanguard Retirement Income Calculator — Best Simple Retirement Check

If you want a quick retirement readiness check without setting up a full planning tool, Vanguard's free calculator is the simplest option here.

What it shows:

  • Estimated monthly income from your retirement savings
  • Estimated Social Security and pension income
  • What you'll need each month in retirement, so you can see the gap

Know the assumptions: results are in today's pre-tax dollars, the calculator assumes you can spend about 4% of your initial nest egg each year, and it uses 3% annual inflation. That makes it a sanity check, not a plan.

Best for: A quick retirement check without committing to a full planning tool.

How to Use These Tools Together

You do not need all five. Here is a simple setup based on your situation:

If you are in your 20s-30s building wealth: Empower for net worth tracking + ProjectionLab's free plan for long-term modeling

If you are in your 40s-50s approaching retirement: Empower + Boldin for serious retirement planning

If you are married or in a partnership: Monarch Money for shared financial management

If you want maximum tax efficiency in retirement: a paid plan — Boldin PlannerPlus or ProjectionLab Premium — since Roth conversion and tax tools are not in either free tier

Best Free Retirement Planning Software in 2027

Of the five tools above, four are built for retirement planning. Side by side:

  • Boldin (formerly NewRetirement) — the deepest retirement modeling: Social Security, Roth conversions, healthcare costs and lifetime taxes. Those features are in PlannerPlus ($168 a year); Basic is free.
  • Empower (formerly Personal Capital) — best for tracking your actual net worth alongside the projection. The free Retirement Planner works from your linked account balances.
  • Vanguard Retirement Income Calculator — best free tool for a quick sanity check: monthly income from savings, Social Security and pension against what you'll need. Strictly simpler than Boldin or Empower.
  • ProjectionLab — best free engine for long-term modeling, with Monte Carlo and historical backtesting in the free plan. Side-by-side scenario comparison requires Premium.

For most people, the right stack is: Empower for monthly tracking, ProjectionLab or Boldin for the deep plan once a year, and Vanguard's calculator as the quick sanity check between formal reviews.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Empower's financial dashboard really free?

Yes. Empower says its dashboard and financial tools are completely free. It makes money from optional paid advisory services, which it promotes to users.

Does ProjectionLab have a free plan?

Yes. The Basic plan is $0 and includes forecasting, Monte Carlo simulations and historical backtesting. Premium, at $129 a year, adds compare mode, multiple plans, Roth conversions and tax tools.

How much does Boldin PlannerPlus cost?

PlannerPlus costs $168 a year, billed annually after a 14-day free trial. The Basic plan is free.

Is Monarch Money free?

No. Monarch has no free plan. Core costs $14.99 a month or $99.99 a year, and the first week is free.

The Bottom Line

You do not need a financial advisor to build a solid financial plan. The free tools above cover net worth tracking, long-term projections and a quick retirement check, and the paid plans are cheap compared with professional advice if you need tax and Roth conversion modeling.

Start with Empower. Connect your accounts. Look at your net worth, your investments and your retirement projection.

That 15 minutes will tell you more about your financial situation than most people learn in a decade.
