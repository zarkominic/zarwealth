The cheapest way to save automatically in 2027 is to skip the subscription: Chime and Ally charge no monthly fee, while Oportun (formerly Digit), Qapital and Acorns charge $3 to $12 a month. At Ally's 3.2% APY, $1,000 earns about $32 a year, less than the cheapest paid plan here ($36).

When saving is automatic, it happens; when it needs a monthly decision, it often does not. Interest rates on savings are variable, so every rate here is dated; look at the live rate before you open anything.

Why Automatic Saving Works

The gap between people who save consistently and people who do not is rarely income. It is system design.

People who save consistently have systems that make saving the default behavior. People who struggle to save rely on willpower and good intentions, both of which are finite and unreliable. An app that moves a little money every payday takes willpower out of the picture.

Does the subscription pay for itself?

This is the weak spot of paid saving apps: you pay a fee to save your own money. Here is the arithmetic using the published prices and rates (simple one-year interest; the balances are examples, not averages):

  • Fee-free baseline: a $1,000 balance at Ally's 3.2% APY earns about $32 a year; $5,000 earns about $160. At Chime's top 4.00% tier, $1,000 earns about $40. At the 0.39% national average, $1,000 earns about $3.90.
  • Qapital Basic: $3 a month is $36 a year, more than the $32 that $1,000 earns at 3.2%. Qapital's pages show no interest rate, so we cannot say the Save account earns anything.
  • Oportun: $5.99 a month is $71.88 a year, or $47.88 a year at the $3.99 yearly-plan price. The App Store listing shows no interest rate.
  • Acorns Silver: $8 a month is $96 a year. Emergency Savings at 3.59% APY (as of 09/22/26) only earns $96 on a balance of about $2,674, so below that the interest does not cover the plan on its own. (Silver also includes other features, so this is only the savings math.) Bronze, at $4 a month, does not include Emergency Savings.

If your goal is simply to put money aside, a free account with automatic transfers does the same job without a fee. A paid plan can make sense only if you will actually use features you cannot get elsewhere.

The Best Automatic Saving Apps in 2027

1. Oportun (formerly Digit): Best for Hands-Off, Algorithm-Timed Saving

Digit is now the Oportun: Savings & Loans app. Its App Store listing says it was formerly known as Digit. The savings feature, Set & Save, learns your income and spending patterns and sets money aside toward your goals when it judges the moment suitable. You can pause saves, move money between goals or transfer it back to your bank. Smart Bills goals can save for recurring bills.

Price: $5.99 a month, or $3.99 a month on a yearly plan, after a 30-day free trial; you can cancel anytime. Savings are FDIC-insured up to $250,000 through partner banks, according to the listing, which does not name them, and Oportun states it is not a bank. We could not verify an interest rate for the savings, and oportun.com blocked automated reading, so we do not quote one.

Worth knowing: on August 10, 2022 the CFPB found that Hello Digit (Digit's former operator) had deceived consumers about its automated savings tool, including overdraft fees caused by its transfers, and fined it $2.7 million (see Sources).

Best for: people who want saving fully automated and are comfortable paying for it.

2. Qapital: Best for Goal-Based Saving

Qapital's Save puts money away automatically once you set a goal, and its Budget feature sets spending money aside the moment you get paid. Monthly prices: Basic $3, Complete $8 (adds investing, a Visa debit card and personalized insights) and Premier $12 (adds Premier Invest Portfolios), with a 30-day free trial.

Qapital is a fintech company, not a bank. Lincoln Savings Bank, Member FDIC, provides the Save and Spend accounts and the debit card, and deposit insurance covers that bank, not Qapital. On Basic, Save funds are held in an umbrella fiduciary account for Qapital users; with a Spend account on Complete or Premier, Save and Spend funds are held separately in your name. Qapital's pages state no interest rate.

Best for: people who want goals and paycheck-based saving set up once and left alone.

3. Acorns: Best for Investing Spare Change

Acorns invests rather than saves: Round-Ups invest the spare change from purchases (a $2.50 purchase invests $0.50). Plans: Bronze $4, Silver $8, Gold $12 a month. All three include Round-Ups, an investment account and an Acorns Later retirement account. Silver and Gold add Emergency Savings, a savings account advertising a variable 3.59% APY as of September 22, 2026 (check the live rate).

Investments are offered through Acorns Advisers, LLC and Acorns Securities, LLC (a FINRA and SIPC member broker-dealer) and are not FDIC insured and can lose value. Acorns Checking and Emergency Savings are provided through Lincoln Savings Bank or nbkc bank, both FDIC members; deposits there are insured up to $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, per ownership category.

Best for: beginners who want to invest spare change and value the bundle. If you only want a savings account, the Silver plan at $96 a year is the costly way to get one.

4. Chime: Best Free Option

Chime's savings account has no monthly fee, but you need a Chime Checking Account to open one. Automatic savings come from Round Ups (purchases rounded up to the next dollar, with the difference moved to savings) and automatic transfers, including a rule that routes 10% of each direct deposit.

The savings APY depends on your tier, effective 10/06/26 and variable: 1.00% standard, 3.00% Chime Plus (a single $200+ deposit or $400+ cumulative in the past 34 days) and 4.00% Chime Prime ($3,000+ in qualifying direct deposits in the past 34 days). Chime is not a bank; banking is provided by The Bancorp Bank, N.A. or Stride Bank, N.A., both FDIC members, and coverage is up to $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, per ownership category. Optional services such as instant transfers can carry fees.

Best for: people who get paid by direct deposit and want automation without a subscription.

5. Ally Savings Buckets: Best for Goal-Based Saving

Ally's online savings account has no monthly fee and no minimum balance or opening deposit. You can split it into up to 30 buckets, each with a goal. Three boosters automate saving: Recurring Transfers (scheduled transfers), Round Ups (spending-account purchases rounded up; a transfer happens once at least $5 has accrued, and each ranges from $5 to $20) and Surprise Savings (safe-to-save money from linked checking accounts).

The APY was 3.2% as of 11/24/26 and is variable. Deposits are FDIC-insured up to the maximum allowed by law. Ally limits certain savings withdrawals and transfers to 10 per statement cycle.

Best for: goal-oriented savers who want several goals in one fee-free account.

How to Stack Automatic Saving Apps

You do not need all five. A simple setup is a fee-free account that does the automating (Ally buckets, or Chime if you have direct deposit), plus a paid app only if you will use something the free option lacks. What to Do With Automatically Saved Money

Automatic saving apps are the collection mechanism; once a balance builds up, check whether it is earning a competitive rate. Compare the rate with the national average (0.39% as of November 16, 2026, per the FDIC) before leaving a large balance where it earns little.

  • Emergency fund: keep it in an insured savings account you can reach quickly.
  • Short-term goals: a savings account or money market account.
  • Long-term goals: investments, which are not FDIC-insured and can lose value.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best app to save money automatically?

For most people, a free one: Ally (buckets, recurring transfers and round-ups, $0 monthly fee) or Chime (round-ups and direct-deposit transfers, $0 for savings). The paid apps charge $3 to $12 a month, which is more than many balances earn in a year.

Are automatic saving apps FDIC insured?

The apps themselves are not banks. Insurance comes from the partner bank that holds the deposit: Lincoln Savings Bank (Qapital, and Acorns Checking and Emergency Savings), nbkc bank (Acorns), The Bancorp Bank and Stride Bank (Chime). Ally is itself an FDIC-insured bank. Investments, such as Acorns Round-Ups, are not FDIC insured and can lose value.

Is it worth paying a monthly fee to save?

Usually not for saving alone. Qapital Basic costs $36 a year and Acorns Silver $96, while $1,000 at 3.2% earns about $32. A paid plan is worth it only if you will use the other features it includes.

What happened to Digit?

Digit is now the Oportun: Savings & Loans app, and its App Store listing says it was formerly known as Digit. It still offers automated saving for $5.99 a month, or $3.99 a month on the yearly plan, after a 30-day free trial.

Do round-ups really add up?

It depends on how often you spend. A purchase of $2.50 rounds up by $0.50, so the amount saved is small per transaction; the value is that it happens without a decision. Ally moves round-ups once at least $5 has accrued.

The Bottom Line

Automatic saving is not about discipline; it is about system design. Pick one account, turn on one automatic rule, and leave it. Be skeptical of paying a subscription to save your own money unless the features are worth more than the fee.
