What Is a High-Yield Savings Account?

A high-yield savings account (HYSA) works like a regular savings account: you deposit money, it earns interest, and you can move it back out to a linked checking account. The difference is the rate. Many online savings accounts pay several times the national average, as the table below shows.

Deposits at an FDIC-insured bank are covered up to $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, for each account ownership category, if the bank fails. A HYSA is not an investment: its rate is variable and can fall as well as rise, and it is a place for money you may need soon, not for long-term growth.

1. Bread Savings: 4.05% APY

Bread Savings is a product of Comenity Capital Bank, Member FDIC. Its page says all Bread Savings APYs are accurate as of 11/28/2026, subject to change at any time, and that the rate may change after you open the account. Opening takes $100 deposited in a single transaction. The page advertises "No maintenance or hidden fees" and says Bread Savings accounts do not charge monthly maintenance fees, but fees may apply to certain optional services (for example $15 for a official check, $25 for an outgoing wire and $5 for a paper statement), and fees may reduce earnings.

2. Marcus by Goldman Sachs: 3.50% APY

Marcus showed 3.50% APY as of November 28, 2026, with no fees and no minimum deposit. It also advertises a +1.00% increase for referring a friend; that is a promotion, not part of the standard rate, and we do not count it in the ranking.

3. SoFi Savings: 3.30% APY with direct deposit

SoFi Bank, N.A. (Member FDIC) pays 3.30% APY (rate as of October 20, 2026) if you have qualifying direct deposit, or deposits of at least $5,000 every 31 days; without either, the APY is 0.80%. New accounts opened with direct deposit before 12/31/27 can add 1.10% for up to 6 months, up to 4.40% in total; that is a temporary promotion, so the 3.30% is the rate that lasts. There are no monthly, overdraft or minimum-balance fees and no minimum deposit. Opening the account also opens a SoFi checking account. Beyond the standard $250,000 FDIC coverage, SoFi says the SoFi Insured Deposit Program provides up to $3 million in additional coverage.

4. Ally Online Savings: 3.2% APY

Ally's page showed 3.2% APY as of November 24, 2026, and calls the rate variable. There is no minimum deposit or minimum balance, and no monthly maintenance fee. Ally also offers savings buckets, which it describes as digital envelopes for organizing savings by goal, while you keep earning interest on your total balance. Expedited delivery costs $15, outgoing domestic wires $20, and the account allows 10 limited withdrawals and transfers per statement cycle.

5. American Express High Yield Savings: 3.20% APY

American Express National Bank (Member FDIC) showed 3.20% APY, accurate as of 11/28/2026 and subject to change at any time. There is no minimum balance and no monthly fee.

6. Capital One 360 Performance Savings: 3.20% APY

Capital One's page showed 3.20% APY on November 28, 2026, with no "as of" date visible, so treat it as the rate shown that day. There is no minimum balance. Discover's online savings page now redirects to this Capital One page (see What changed).

The ranking is by standard APY only. Bread's rate needs a $100 opening deposit, and SoFi's depends on direct deposit or $5,000 in monthly deposits; the others have no minimum, so the right account depends on how you will use it.

How Much More Could You Earn?

APY already includes compounding, so a balance that stays put for a year earns roughly the APY times the balance, as long as the rate does not change. On $10,000:

  • At the 0.39% national average (FDIC, November 16, 2026): about $39 a year
  • At 3.2% (Ally, Amex and Capital One): about $320 a year
  • At 3.50% (Marcus): about $350 a year
  • At 4.05% (Bread Savings): about $405 a year

That is a gap of roughly $281 to $366 a year on $10,000, and it only lasts as long as those variable rates do. If the bank cuts the rate, your earnings fall with it.

How to Compare High-Yield Savings Accounts

  • Look at the date next to the APY. A rate with no "as of" date may already have changed.
  • Check the minimums. Opening deposit and ongoing balance requirements differ (Bread asks for $100 to open; Ally does not).
  • Check fees and limits. Monthly fees can eat into interest. Also read the transfer and withdrawal limits and the cost of wires or expedited delivery.
  • Confirm FDIC insurance. Look for the bank's FDIC membership, and remember the $250,000 limit applies per depositor, per bank, per ownership category.
  • Remember the rate is variable. It can change after you open the account, so recheck it every few months.

What to Use a High-Yield Savings Account For

HYSAs are not for long-term wealth building; that is what investing is for. They suit:

  • Emergency fund: liquid, insured, earning interest
  • Short-term goals: vacation, car down payment, home deposit
  • Money you need within 1-3 years: anything you cannot afford to lose to market volatility

For money you will not need for 5+ years, consider investing it instead. If you also want to compare insured accounts that pay interest and come with check-writing, see our guides to money market accounts and no-fee checking accounts.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are high-yield savings accounts safe?

At an FDIC-insured bank, deposits are insured up to $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, for each account ownership category, if the bank fails. You can check coverage with the FDIC's Electronic Deposit Insurance Estimator (EDIE).

Are high-yield savings rates fixed?

No. The accounts here show variable rates, and Bread Savings says the rate may change after the account is opened. Certificates of deposit are the product that locks a rate for a set term.

How much higher is a high-yield account than the national average?

The FDIC's national average savings rate was 0.39% on November 16, 2026. Bread Savings' 4.05% is about 10 times that, Marcus's 3.50% about 9 times, and the 3.20% to 3.30% accounts about 8 times.

Can I lose money in a high-yield savings account?

Your balance does not fall with markets, but fees, if any, can reduce earnings, and balances above the insurance limit are not covered. The rate can also fall.

The Bottom Line

Leaving an emergency fund in an account that pays the national average means earning about $39 a year on $10,000. The accounts above showed 3.20% to 4.05% on November 28, 2026, which is about $320 to $405 a year, but those rates move, so compare the live APY, the minimums and the fees before you choose.
