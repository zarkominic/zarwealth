How Cashback Apps Work

Retailers and brands pay cashback apps a commission or a promotion fee when you buy through them. The apps share part of that money with you as cash back, points or rewards. You pay the same price either way; the app only gets credit if it can track your purchase.

That tracking is the part people miss. A browser extension, a click-through link, a scanned receipt or a linked store account is how each app proves you bought something. If two apps try to track the same online purchase, usually only one gets credit. More on that below.

The 5 Best Cashback Apps for 2027 (Reviewed)

1. Rakuten: Best for Online Shopping

Rakuten is a cash back shopping portal; its homepage advertises 3,500+ stores.

How it works: start your shopping trip from Rakuten's site or app, or install the browser extension and activate Cash Back when you land on a partner store. Shop normally. Once the store confirms the purchase, the Cash Back moves into your balance.

Cash back rates: they vary by store and change often, including short promotions. Rakuten shows the current rate on each store page; check it before you buy.

Welcome bonus: Rakuten's welcome bonus terms require at least $25 in qualifying purchases within 90 days of signing up, and purchases on Amazon.com don't count. The bonus amount changes with the promotion running when you join, so check it on the sign-up page.

Payout: every three months. Rakuten lists payment dates of February 15, May 15, August 15 and November 15, and you need $5 or more in confirmed Cash Back to get paid; a smaller balance carries over. You choose PayPal, check, Bilt Points or Samsung Cash.

Best for: anyone who shops online regularly at big-name retailers.

2. Ibotta: Best for Grocery Cashback

Ibotta specializes in grocery and everyday purchases, in store and online.

How it works: add offers to your list in the app before you shop, buy the products, then either upload your paper receipt or shop with a linked store account. In-store receipts must be paper receipts printed at the register and sent within 7 days of purchase.

Cash back amounts: set per offer, usually a fixed amount per product rather than a percentage, and they change constantly. The app shows the amount on each offer.

Linked accounts: at Walmart you can link your Walmart account and earn automatically in store and on pickup and delivery orders. Kroger and its banner stores (Ralphs, King Soopers, Fred Meyer and others) are not eligible for automatic cash back; there you upload a photo of the paper receipt.

Welcome bonus: Ibotta offers welcome bonuses to brand-new users, but the amount and requirements change regularly. The app's Bonuses page shows the current one.

Cashing out: you need at least $20 in earnings, at least one redeemed offer, and a 7-day wait after your first redemption. Then you can withdraw to a participating bank, PayPal or a gift card; Ibotta says funds take 0 to 5 days depending on the method.

Watch out for: account maintenance fees. If you have no activity for 180 days, Ibotta deducts $3.99 from your balance for every 30 days the account stays inactive. Ibotta says you avoid the fee by redeeming an offer, completing a Gaming Offer, or receiving credit for a Referral Bonus at least once every 180 days.

Best for: grocery shoppers willing to add offers before each trip.

3. PayPal Honey: Best for Automatic Coupon Finding

Honey is a coupon finder first. Its browser extension looks for coupon codes at checkout on supported sites, and at participating stores you can also earn rewards points. Honey is part of PayPal, and its help center now calls the rewards PayPal Rewards points (Honey's site still uses the older name Honey Gold in places).

How it works: install the extension, shop as usual, and let it test codes at checkout. Where a store offers rewards, activate them before you buy.

Redeeming: U.S. members need 1,000 points for a gift card. With a linked U.S. personal PayPal account in good standing, you can also use points toward a purchase through PayPal checkout. Redemptions are final.

Watch out for: it is a competing extension as far as Rakuten is concerned (see the stacking section).

Best for: online shoppers who forget to look for coupon codes.

4. Capital One Shopping: Best Alternative to Honey

Capital One Shopping works much like Honey: a browser extension that looks for coupon codes and earns Shopping Rewards at participating stores. Capital One says it is free for everyone and you don't need to be a Capital One customer to use it.

How it works: install the extension, shop, and collect Shopping Rewards on eligible purchases. Rewards are exchanged for e-gift cards once you have at least $5 worth.

Good to know: under its terms, unused Shopping Rewards on a valid account don't expire, but they are forfeited if the account is closed, so redeem before you delete it.

Best for: a second coupon finder. Like Honey, it competes with Rakuten for tracking, so don't let both credit the same purchase.

5. Fetch: Best for Receipt Scanning

Fetch (formerly Fetch Rewards) gives you points for scanning almost any store receipt, not only specific products.

How it works: shop anywhere in the U.S. or Puerto Rico, then snap the receipt in the Fetch app within 14 days. Every receipt earns at least 25 points; receipts with partner products, offers or bonus events earn more.

Redeeming: Fetch says most rewards need at least 3,000 points, and rewards are mostly digital gift cards.

Watch out for: if your account is inactive for 90 consecutive days, your points expire. Snapping one receipt keeps it active. There is also a limit of 35 receipts in 8 days.

Best for: people who want something back on every grocery trip without planning around offers.

How to Stack Cashback for Maximum Returns

Stacking means earning from more than one layer on the same purchase. The layers that combine cleanly are different kinds of reward: one shopping portal, your credit card's rewards, and the store's own loyalty program. What does not combine cleanly is two cash back extensions on one online order.

Rakuten says clicking a link from another coupon or rewards site after activating its extension disables Rakuten Cash Back, and it recommends disabling or uninstalling competing extensions. PayPal Honey and Capital One Shopping are exactly that kind of extension. So for each online order, decide which one gets the credit.

Hypothetical online example (the rates are made up to show the math; check the live rate in the app):

  1. Rakuten Cash Back: if the store page shows 5%, a $150 order earns $7.50
  2. Credit card rewards: a card earning 2% adds $3.00
  3. Coupon code: any discount comes off the price, but Rakuten warns that codes from other coupon or rewards sites can switch off its tracking
  4. Total in this example: $10.50 back on $150, before any coupon

Pair the portal with a rewards credit card for the card layer.

Grocery stack:

  1. Ibotta: add offers before you shop, then upload the paper receipt (or use a linked account where available)
  2. Store loyalty program: the store's own points or digital coupons
  3. Credit card: your card's rewards on the grocery charge
  4. Fetch: snap the same receipt for points within 14 days

How much this adds up to depends entirely on what you buy and which offers are live, so treat any monthly figure you see online, including in older versions of this article, as a guess. If you want a broader system for automating your savings, see the best apps to save money automatically.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best cashback app in 2027?

Rakuten is the best starting point for online shopping: 3,500+ stores, and it pays in cash through PayPal or check every three months once you have $5 in confirmed Cash Back. For groceries, Ibotta is the stronger choice. Most people benefit from using both.

Are cashback apps worth it?

Yes, with two caveats. First, never change what you buy just to earn cash back; the apps only save money on purchases you were already going to make. Second, check how each app pays: Rakuten and Ibotta can pay cash, while PayPal Honey, Capital One Shopping and Fetch pay mainly in gift cards or points. Also mind the fine print: Ibotta deducts $3.99 every 30 days from the balance of an account inactive for 180 days, and Fetch points expire after 90 days of inactivity.

Which cashback app pays the most?

There is no fixed answer, because rates change by store and by week. For online shopping, compare the live rate on Rakuten's store page before you buy. For groceries, Ibotta pays per product, so what you earn depends on buying the products on offer. Fetch pays something on almost every receipt, starting at 25 points.

Can I use multiple cashback apps at the same time?

Yes, as long as they track purchases in different ways. Ibotta and Fetch both work from receipts, so they can sit side by side, and a rewards credit card works with any of them. For online orders, use one cash back extension per purchase: Rakuten says competing coupon or rewards extensions can disable its Cash Back, so don't let Honey or Capital One Shopping activate on an order you started from Rakuten.

Are cashback apps safe?

The apps listed here are established businesses. Ibotta has traded on the New York Stock Exchange under the symbol IBTA since April 2024, and Honey is part of PayPal. The main privacy trade-off: these apps see your purchases (receipts, linked store accounts or browsing on partner stores) to verify cash back. Read each app's privacy policy if that is a concern.

How long does it take to get paid?

It varies by app. Rakuten pays on February 15, May 15, August 15 and November 15, for Cash Back the store has already confirmed. Ibotta says withdrawals take 0 to 5 days once you meet the $20 threshold and other requirements. PayPal Honey, Capital One Shopping and Fetch let you redeem in the app once you reach their minimums (1,000 points, $5 in rewards and 3,000 points respectively).

Rakuten vs PayPal Honey: How Do They Compare?

Rakuten and PayPal Honey are the two most-searched names, and they solve different problems:

  • Main job: Rakuten is a cash back portal across 3,500+ stores. Honey is a coupon finder that also offers rewards points at participating stores.
  • Sign-up bonus: Rakuten has a welcome bonus that requires $25 in qualifying purchases within 90 days; the amount varies by promotion.
  • Coupon engine: Honey looks for and tests coupon codes at checkout. Rakuten offers coupons alongside Cash Back on its store pages.
  • Payout: Rakuten pays every three months by PayPal, check, Bilt Points or Samsung Cash. Honey pays in PayPal Rewards points, redeemable for gift cards (1,000 points minimum in the U.S.) or through PayPal checkout.
  • Best for: Rakuten for high-volume online shoppers; Honey for passive coupon savings.

Can you run both? You can install both, but don't let both act on the same order. Rakuten says another coupon or rewards site or extension can disable its Cash Back. If an order has a large Rakuten rate, start from Rakuten and skip Honey's rewards on that checkout.

The Bottom Line

Cashback apps do not make you rich. They do put money back on purchases you were already going to make, as long as you stay on top of the minimums and the fine print.

Start with Rakuten and Ibotta: both are free, both can pay cash, and together they cover most online and grocery spending. Add Fetch for every receipt. Use a coupon extension if you like, but one cash back tracker per online order.
