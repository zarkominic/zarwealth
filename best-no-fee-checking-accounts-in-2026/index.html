What to Look for in a No-Fee Checking Account

Zero monthly maintenance fee: the baseline requirement.

No minimum balance: fees triggered by falling below a minimum are still fees.

Overdraft policy: check whether the account charges an overdraft fee, declines the purchase, or offers an opt-in cushion with conditions.

ATM access: look at the fee-free network size and what happens at other ATMs. Some accounts reimburse the other ATM's fee (Axos, Schwab, Ally up to $10 per statement cycle); others only avoid their own fee.

Early direct deposit: Chime, Capital One and Axos each say direct deposits can arrive up to two days early, depending on when your payer sends the funds.

FDIC insurance: the FDIC says deposits are insured to at least $250,000 at each FDIC-insured bank, and only at an FDIC-insured bank. A fintech app is not a bank; it needs a partner bank holding your money, and its own site should name that bank.

The Best No-Fee Checking Accounts in 2027

1. Chime Checking: Best Overall

Chime says it is a financial technology company, not a bank. Its checking account is held at The Bancorp Bank, N.A. or Stride Bank, N.A., both Members FDIC, and your funds are insured up to $250,000 through those partner banks. Chime lists no monthly service fee, no minimum balance and no overdraft fee: a transaction that exceeds your balance or SpotMe limit is declined.

Standout features:

  • SpotMe: optional overdraft of up to $200 with no fees. Eligibility requires $200 or more in qualifying direct deposits each month, and the initial limit may be as low as $20.
  • Direct deposit up to two days early
  • 47,000+ fee-free ATMs; Allpoint, Visa Plus Alliance and 7-Eleven ATMs are fee-free, and other ATMs cost $2.50 per transaction plus any fee the operator charges
  • No APY is stated for checking

Best for: people who want a simple fee-free account and do not need interest on checking.

2. Ally Bank Spending Account: Best for a Full-Service Online Bank

Ally's checking account is now labeled the Spending Account on its site. Ally lists no monthly maintenance fee, no overdraft fees and no minimum balance. As of November 28, 2026 it pays 0.10% APY on balances under $15,000 and 0.25% APY at $15,000 or more; the rates are variable and may change after you open the account.

Standout features:

  • 75,000+ no-fee ATMs, plus up to $10 per statement cycle reimbursed for fees at other ATMs
  • Optional Overdraft Transfer Service and opt-in CoverDraft, which Ally describes as protection against overdraft items
  • No branches, with phone support around the clock according to Ally
  • Ally Bank, Member FDIC

Best for: people who want some interest and reimbursement for the occasional out-of-network ATM fee.

4. Current: Not Ranked This Year

Current says it is a financial technology company, not an FDIC-insured bank; banking services are provided by Cross River Bank, Member FDIC, and pass-through FDIC coverage up to $250,000 applies only if certain conditions are met. Its site advertises fee-free overdraft (you need at least $200 in eligible direct deposits over the preceding 35 days), 40,000+ fee-free ATMs, and a $2.50 out-of-network ATM fee for Build Card users. We removed Current from the ranking because the page we checked does not state a monthly fee (its fee summary says some fees may apply), and the Savings Pod rate it shows (up to 4.00%) carries an effective date of August 1, 2023, so we cannot present it as a current APY.

5. Capital One 360 Checking: Replaces Discover Cashback Debit

Discover's online banking page now says "Discover is part of Capital One", and its checking page redirects to Capital One's 360 Checking page, which does not mention a cash back debit feature. We removed the old Discover pick and its 1% cash back claim, and we are not recommending a cash back debit account this year.

Capital One lists no monthly fee, no minimum balance and no overdraft fees on 360 Checking. As of November 28, 2026 the APY is 0.10% in every balance tier (variable).

Standout features:

  • 70,000+ fee-free Capital One, MoneyPass and Allpoint ATMs (count last updated September 18, 2026); Capital One does not add a fee at other ATMs, but the ATM owner may
  • No-fee overdraft is opt-in and requires regular deposits of at least $250 in two of the previous three calendar months, so new accounts do not have it at first
  • Early paycheck up to two days with direct deposit, depending on your payer
  • Branches and Capital One Cafes; Cafes do not provide all branch services
  • Capital One, N.A., Member FDIC

Best for: people who want a no-fee account with some in-person support.

6. Axos Checking: Best for ATM Users and Interest

Axos's own site now lists the account as Axos Checking (our earlier name, Rewards Checking, no longer appears on its checking page). It lists no monthly maintenance, balance, opening or overdraft fees. As of November 28, 2026 it pays 1.00% APY for each monthly statement cycle in which the account receives at least $1 in qualifying direct deposits; without that, the base rate is 0.00% APY. The rate is variable and can change as often as daily.

Standout features:

  • 95,000+ ATMs nationwide with unlimited domestic ATM fee reimbursements
  • Paycheck up to two days early with direct deposit
  • Deposits are FDIC-insured at Axos Bank (certificate 35546)
  • Axos ONE, a checking and savings bundle, is a separate product: its checking pays a 0.00% base APY or 0.51% boosted, and it reimburses ATM fees only up to $10

Best for: people who set up direct deposit and want both interest and unlimited ATM fee reimbursements.

7. Schwab Bank Investor Checking: Best for Investors and Travelers

Schwab's page lists no monthly service fees, no overdraft fees (Schwab Bank does not charge a fee for overdraft services), no foreign transaction fees and unlimited ATM fee rebates for cash withdrawals worldwide. The account must be linked to a new or existing Schwab One brokerage account, and its overdraft protection comes from that account: Schwab says protection using margin sources may create a margin loan that can be subject to daily interest charges, margin calls or potential liquidation of securities. It pays 0.01% APY as of November 28, 2026 (variable, with a $0.01 minimum balance to earn it), which is below the 0.07% national rate for interest checking, so it is a fee play, not an interest play. Deposits are FDIC-insured at Charles Schwab Bank, SSB; the linked brokerage account is not FDIC-insured.

Best for: Schwab brokerage customers and frequent travelers.

How to Switch Checking Accounts Without Disruption

Switching banks is simpler than most people expect, but give it a few weeks.

Step 1: Open the new account. Most of the accounts above have no minimum to open.

Step 2: Update your direct deposit with your employer. Capital One notes it may take two cycles for direct deposits to take effect.

Step 3: Update automatic bill payments one at a time as you confirm each one has shifted to the new account.

Step 4: Keep your old account open with a small balance for a couple of months to catch any missed automatic payments.

Step 5: Close the old account once all transactions have migrated successfully.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best no-fee checking account?

It depends on what you value. Axos Checking pays 1.00% APY with at least $1 in monthly direct deposit and reimburses domestic ATM fees without a cap. Ally pays 0.10% to 0.25% and has 75,000+ no-fee ATMs. Capital One 360 offers branches and Cafes, and Chime is the simplest fee-free app. All list $0 monthly fees.

Is Chime a bank, and is my money FDIC-insured?

No, Chime says it is a financial technology company, not a bank. Your checking account is held at The Bancorp Bank, N.A. or Stride Bank, N.A., both Members FDIC, and funds are insured up to $250,000 through them.

Do no-fee checking accounts charge overdraft fees?

The five accounts here list no overdraft fees, but the cushion differs. Chime declines purchases beyond your balance or SpotMe limit. Capital One's no-fee overdraft requires regular deposits of at least $250. Ally's CoverDraft is opt-in and not guaranteed. Read each provider's terms before relying on it.

Is the interest on checking worth chasing?

Usually not by itself. The FDIC's national rate for interest checking was 0.07% on November 16, 2026. On a $2,500 balance, 1.00% is about $25 a year before taxes. Rates are variable, so check the current rate before you open an account.
