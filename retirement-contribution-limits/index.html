401(k), IRA and HSA Contribution Limits for 2027

The 2027 limits at a glance: 401(k) $25,500, IRA $7,500, HSA $4,500 or $9,000, with catch-ups, income phase-outs and 2026 side by side.

For 2027 you can put up to $25,500 of your own pay into a 401(k), 403(b), governmental 457 or the Thrift Savings Plan, and up to $7,500 into an IRA (traditional and Roth combined). The 401(k) limit rose $1,000 from 2026; the IRA limit did not change.

The IRS announced the 2027 retirement plan limits on November 12, 2026, and the 2027 HSA limits earlier in the year. This page puts 2026 and 2027 side by side and is updated every November.

401(k), 403(b), 457 and TSP limits

The $25,500 limit applies to what you contribute from your own paycheck, whether pre-tax or Roth, across all of your 401(k), 403(b), governmental 457 and TSP accounts combined. Employer matching and profit sharing do not count against it.

If you are 50 or older, you can add $8,500, for $34,000 in total. If you turn 60, 61, 62 or 63 during 2027, the higher catch-up of $11,750 applies instead of the $8,500, for $37,250 in total. The higher catch-up comes from SECURE 2.0 and is still in force for 2027. It applies to most 401(k), 403(b) and governmental 457 plans, but the plan has to offer catch-ups.

The section 415(c) limit caps everything going into your account in a year: your deferrals plus the employer's contributions. For 2027 it is $75,000, and catch-up contributions are on top of that. Most people never get near it unless they have a generous employer contribution or an after-tax feature.

Do high earners have to make catch-up contributions as Roth?

Yes. Under SECURE 2.0, the IRS says the rule has applied since 2026: if your prior-year wages with the employer sponsoring the plan (Social Security wages, Box 3 of Form W-2) were more than the threshold, your catch-up contributions must be made as Roth contributions, meaning after tax. The final regulations (T.D. 10033) generally apply to contributions in taxable years beginning after December 31, 2026, so 2027 is the first year they apply formally; plans could use a reasonable, good-faith interpretation of the law for 2026. The threshold is $150,000 of 2025 wages for 2026 contributions and $155,000 of 2026 wages for 2027 contributions. Below the threshold you can still choose pre-tax or Roth catch-ups if your plan offers both. The Roth catch-up rule does not apply to SIMPLE IRAs or SEPs, and your regular $25,500 can still be pre-tax. If your plan has no Roth option, high earners cannot make catch-up contributions at all.

IRA limits and income phase-outs

The IRA limit stays at $7,500 for 2027, plus $1,100 if you are 50 or older, for $8,600. That is a combined limit across all your traditional and Roth IRAs, and it can never exceed your taxable compensation for the year. A spouse without income can contribute based on the working spouse's income when you file jointly.

Income decides whether you can contribute to a Roth IRA, and whether a traditional IRA contribution is tax deductible if you or your spouse are covered by a workplace plan. The amount you can contribute shrinks gradually across the phase-out range and reaches zero at the top of it.

  • Roth IRA: $158,000 to $173,000 for single filers and heads of household, $250,000 to $260,000 for married couples filing jointly.
  • Traditional IRA deduction, covered by a workplace plan: $83,000 to $93,000 if single, $133,000 to $153,000 if married filing jointly.
  • Traditional IRA deduction, not covered but your spouse is: $250,000 to $260,000.

Anyone with earned income can contribute to a traditional IRA regardless of income. The phase-outs only affect the deduction. Not sure which type suits you? See Roth vs. traditional IRA and what a Roth IRA is.

HSA limits

You can contribute to a health savings account only while covered by a qualifying high-deductible health plan (HDHP). For 2027 the limit is $4,500 with self-only coverage and $9,000 with family coverage. If you are 55 or older, add $1,000 (a statutory amount that does not change with inflation), for $5,500 self-only. If both spouses are 55 or older, each needs their own HSA to take their own $1,000.

For 2027 an HDHP must have an annual deductible of at least $1,750 for self-only or $3,500 for family coverage, and an out-of-pocket maximum of no more than $8,700 for self-only or $17,400 for family coverage. Employer contributions count toward the limit.

SIMPLE IRA limits

Employees of small businesses with a SIMPLE IRA or SIMPLE 401(k) can defer $17,500 in 2027. Certain employers with a SIMPLE plan can allow a higher $19,100. The catch-up is $4,000 at 50 and older, or $5,250 for ages 60 to 63.

What changes from 2026 to 2027, and what to do

  • The 401(k) limit rises $1,000 to $25,500, and the age-50 catch-up rises $500 to $8,500. To hit the new maximum, raise your contribution percentage in January rather than waiting for a raise. After your first few paychecks, check your year-to-date total against the limit.
  • The IRA limit does not change. You still have until the tax filing deadline in April 2028, not counting extensions, to make 2027 IRA contributions. Contributions for 2026 can be made until the April 2027 deadline.
  • Roth IRA and IRA deduction phase-outs move up, so someone just over the line in 2026 may qualify in 2027.
  • The Roth catch-up wage threshold rises $5,000 to $155,000, measured on your 2026 wages with your employer. The rule itself has applied since 2026, and the final regulations take formal effect in 2027. If you are 50 or older and above the threshold, your 2027 catch-up dollars are taxed up front. Check that your plan offers a Roth option.
  • The HSA limits rise $100 for self-only and $250 for family coverage.

Frequently asked questions

What is the 401(k) contribution limit for 2027?

$25,500 for your own contributions, up from $24,500 in 2026. With the age-50 catch-up of $8,500 the total is $34,000, and ages 60 to 63 can contribute up to $37,250 with the $11,750 higher catch-up.

What is the IRA contribution limit for 2027?

$7,500, the same as 2026, or $8,600 if you are 50 or older. The limit covers all traditional and Roth IRAs combined.

When is the deadline to contribute for 2027?

For an IRA, the due date of your 2027 tax return, not counting extensions, which is April 2028 for most people. You can also contribute for 2027 starting January 1, 2027. 401(k) contributions come out of your paycheck, so they must be made through payroll by the end of the plan year (check your plan's rules).

Can I contribute to both a 401(k) and an IRA in 2027?

Yes. The limits are separate, so you can contribute $25,500 to a 401(k) and $7,500 to an IRA. Being covered by a workplace plan can reduce whether your traditional IRA contribution is deductible at higher incomes, see the phase-outs above.

Does the employer match count toward the 401(k) limit?

No. The $25,500 applies to your own deferrals. Employer contributions count only toward the $75,000 total limit.

Can I have an HSA and contribute to a 401(k) and IRA too?

Yes, the HSA limit is separate from the 401(k) and IRA limits. You just need to be covered by a qualifying high-deductible health plan to contribute to the HSA.

When will the 2028 limits be announced?

The IRS has announced the retirement plan limits in the fall (the 2027 limits came out November 12, 2026), and the HSA limits earlier in the year. This page is updated each November.
